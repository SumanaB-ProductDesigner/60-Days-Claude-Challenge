# 60-Days-Claude-Challenge
I tried something different today — I asked Claude (Anthropic's AI) to build me an "AI Personality Profile" based on how I actually work, think, and use AI. Not a personality quiz. An honest reflection of my process.
What started as a simple prompt turned into one of the most interesting conversations I've had with an AI tool.
The first draft Claude gave me was good — but not me. It assumed I brainstorm and loop through ideas non-linearly. I corrected it: I follow the design thinking process step by step, foundation-first. Empathise and Define get locked in early, and everything I build after that — no matter how advanced — stays anchored to that original idea. I don't scrap the foundation. I build on it.

What struck me was how Claude didn't just accept the correction and move on. Each time I pushed back, it actually integrated the nuance into every other part of the profile — my strengths, my weaknesses, even what I need to improve to get better with AI. It felt less like editing text and more like refining a mirror until it actually reflected how I think.

What genuinely impressed me was the speed without losing substance. What would've taken me hours of writing, restructuring, and second-guessing myself got compressed into a focused back-and-forth — but none of it felt shortcut. Every manual correction I made remained intact in the final output; nothing was smoothed over or generalised away. It sped up the process, not the thinking.
We went back and forth multiple times before it got the working style exactly right — and honestly, that back-and-forth was the most valuable part. It made me articulate something I "knew" about myself but had never actually put into words: that my discipline isn't about moving fast, it's about not skipping steps.

By the end, I had a full profile — working style, strengths, weaknesses, an AI-user archetype, career paths, even a cinematic "character" description — plus a set of visuals I could turn into LinkedIn posts. Not generic AI output. Something built with me, not for me.

As someone working toward becoming an AI-native product designer, I found this a small but real preview of what that looks like in practice: AI as a thinking partner that speeds up articulation without diluting it.

If you haven't tried something like this with Claude yet, I'd recommend it. Not for the novelty — for the clarity it forces out of you.
